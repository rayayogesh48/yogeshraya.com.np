# Karobar: Redesigning a Bookkeeping App for Small Businesses

## Making Business Bookkeeping Easier

Running a small business means managing more than sales. Business owners need to track daily income, record supplier purchases, monitor customer credit, manage inventory, and understand whether their business is performing well. For many small merchants, these responsibilities compete with the time they need to serve customers and run daily operations.

Traditional bookkeeping methods often rely on notebooks, paper bills, spreadsheets, and disconnected digital tools. Small business accounting software can bring these records together, but only when the experience is simple enough to become part of a merchant's routine.

That's the challenge behind the Karobar app redesign. Karobar is a mobile-first bookkeeping and business management application designed for micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs). The product helps business owners manage financial records and everyday business operations from their phones.

As the product evolved, the opportunity was to improve more than its visual interface. The experience needed to make bookkeeping easier to start, financial information easier to understand, and essential business tasks easier to complete.

- **Product:** Karobar, a mobile bookkeeping and business management app.
- **Audience:** Small business owners, retailers, wholesalers, and MSMEs.
- **Role:** Product Designer responsible for UX research synthesis, information architecture, onboarding, dashboard design, and transaction workflows.
- **Scope:** Mobile app UX redesign, financial dashboard improvements, inventory management, and cross-platform product architecture.

The central design question was: **How can a bookkeeping app help small businesses maintain better financial records without making daily operations more complicated?**

## The Problem: Why Small Businesses Struggle With Digital Bookkeeping

The biggest challenge in small business bookkeeping isn't always a lack of software. It's the effort required to use it consistently. Research conducted for Karobar highlighted several barriers that influenced the redesign.

### Manual financial records take time to maintain

Recording every sale, purchase, payment, and expense requires consistent effort. For merchants who spend most of their day serving customers, detailed bookkeeping can feel like an additional job. When maintaining records becomes too time-consuming, important information can end up scattered across multiple places.

Karobar needed to make financial recordkeeping more accessible without assuming every merchant was ready to adopt a detailed accounting workflow.

### Accounting software can feel difficult to get started with

The previous onboarding process collected several business details before users could access the app. Some information was necessary for a complete business profile, but it wasn't all essential to start recording transactions.

This created an opportunity to simplify the registration experience, reduce initial friction, and help new users reach the product's core value sooner.

### Financial dashboards need to answer practical questions

A business dashboard is only useful when its information helps users make decisions. Small business owners need to understand how much money they have, what they need to collect, what they owe, and how sales and expenses are changing.

When financial statistics, reports, and frequently used actions are separated across different sections, users must spend more time navigating the app to find answers.

### Inventory management requires business-specific flexibility

Retailers and wholesalers don't always manage products in the same way. A business might need different selling prices, custom measurement units, stock alerts, or better organization of customers and suppliers.

A bookkeeping app that cannot accommodate these requirements risks becoming less useful as a business grows. The challenge was to create a more intuitive business management app without sacrificing the depth required for real-world operations.

## UX Research: What the Data Revealed About Small Business Bookkeeping

The redesign was informed by research into how businesses maintained financial records, what prevented consistent bookkeeping, and which digital tools they already used. The findings challenged an important assumption: that every small business would naturally want to record every transaction in detail.

### Daily transaction summaries were more common than detailed records

The research report found that **72%** of its surveyed audience recorded daily transaction totals, while **17%** recorded every transaction. This suggested that many merchants approached bookkeeping at the end-of-day level rather than documenting each individual transaction.

For the product, that meant reducing the effort required to record and review business activity was an important design consideration.

### Time and knowledge were significant barriers

The research identified several challenges:

- **35%** cited a lack of time as a reason for not maintaining financial records properly.
- **35%** reported a lack of knowledge about maintaining records.
- **50%** faced difficulties keeping accurate records across bills, ledgers, and digital notes.

These findings pointed toward a practical UX principle: make common bookkeeping tasks easier to understand and complete. Instead of expecting users to learn a complicated accounting workflow immediately, the product needed to help them build better recordkeeping habits over time.

### Mobile accessibility and connectivity mattered

Only **30%** of businesses in the research sample had a dedicated internet connection. This made mobile-first usability, efficient interactions, and consideration for intermittent connectivity important constraints for the product experience. The research also reinforced the need to design for merchants with different levels of digital familiarity.

### The design implication

The research suggested that a successful small business bookkeeping app needs to balance three things:

1. **Simplicity:** Make routine financial tasks easy to complete.
2. **Visibility:** Help business owners understand their financial position quickly.
3. **Flexibility:** Support more advanced business needs without overwhelming beginners.

These principles guided the redesign decisions that followed.

*Research note: The source report does not specify its sample size. The percentages above describe the surveyed audience and should not be interpreted as representative estimates for every small business.*

## UX Design Decisions: Simplifying the Bookkeeping Experience

The redesign focused on reducing unnecessary friction while making important business information and actions easier to find.

### 1. Simplify onboarding before introducing advanced features

**The challenge:** How much information should users provide before they can start using the app?

The previous onboarding experience required multiple business details upfront. While useful for creating a complete profile, not every field was necessary for a first session.

The redesigned approach prioritized essential information:

- Profile type: Personal or Business.
- Business name.
- Business category.

Language selection and alternative sign-in methods were also part of the improved onboarding direction. Additional profile information could be completed later through a profile-completion banner on the homepage.

This approach separated initial activation from full account setup. Rather than asking users to configure everything immediately, the experience was designed to help them get started and complete their profile at their own pace.

**UX principle:** Reduce the time and effort required to reach the product's core value.

### 2. Redesign the financial dashboard around everyday business needs

**The challenge:** How can an accounting dashboard communicate useful information without overwhelming users?

A dashboard can easily become crowded with metrics, graphs, and secondary actions. But showing more information doesn't automatically make a product easier to use.

The redesigned Karobar homepage brought essential business statistics into one place:

- To Receive.
- To Give.
- Sales.
- Purchases.
- Expenses.
- Total Balance.

Business performance graphs provided additional context for sales, purchases, income, and expenses. Quick transaction entry and direct access to reports helped connect financial monitoring with everyday business actions.

This created a clearer relationship between the dashboard and the tasks users needed to perform. The dashboard was no longer just a place to view numbers. It became the starting point for managing business activity.

**UX principle:** Prioritize financial clarity and useful actions over information density.

### 3. Make business reports easier to discover

**The challenge:** How can users access detailed financial information without unnecessary navigation?

Business owners often need to review transaction history, sales, purchases, and expenses to understand their financial performance. Previously, reports were located in the sidebar, adding navigation steps between the homepage and detailed business information.

The redesign brought reports closer to the homepage and improved reporting options. The goal was to make financial analysis a more natural part of the experience rather than a feature users had to search for.

**UX principle:** Reduce navigation friction for frequently used features.

### 4. Design inventory management for different business models

**The challenge:** How can one product support retailers, wholesalers, and businesses with different inventory requirements?

A basic inventory list isn't enough when businesses sell products at different prices or use different measurement units. The redesigned inventory experience introduced support for wholesale pricing and MRP, customizable item units, and low-stock alerts. Party categorization also helped businesses organize customers and suppliers more effectively.

These capabilities made the business management app more adaptable to different operating models. The objective was not to add complexity for its own sake. It was to give businesses the flexibility they needed while keeping common workflows approachable.

**UX principle:** Support business-specific needs without making the core experience unnecessarily complicated.

### 5. Make transaction management more consistent

**The challenge:** How can the app make daily sales, purchases, and financial records easier to manage?

Transaction entry is central to any bookkeeping app. If the workflow feels cumbersome, users have less reason to maintain their records consistently.

The redesigned transaction experience supported sales, purchases, returns, payments, expenses, other income, and quotations. Search and filtering helped users locate existing records, while automatically incrementing transaction numbers and custom prefixes supported more organized invoice management. Quotation support also allowed businesses to prepare price estimates before finalizing a sale.

These improvements addressed both sides of transaction management: recording new business activity and finding existing records later.

**UX principle:** Make the most important workflow predictable, organized, and easy to repeat.

## The Solution: A More Intuitive Small Business Management App

The redesign connected onboarding, bookkeeping, reporting, and inventory management into a more cohesive experience.

### Simplified mobile app onboarding

Users could begin with essential profile information instead of completing every business detail upfront. Language selection and alternative sign-in options supported the broader goal of making the app easier to access, while the profile-completion banner provided a path to finish setup later.

### A centralized business dashboard

The homepage brought financial summaries, business insights, quick entry, reports, shortcuts, and notifications into a more unified experience. This gave users a clearer starting point for understanding business performance and reaching common tasks.

### Structured bookkeeping workflows

Transaction management was organized around the financial activities businesses perform every day. Search, filters, quotation creation, and invoice numbering improvements supported more consistent recordkeeping.

### Flexible inventory and party management

Wholesale pricing, MRP, customizable units, low-stock alerts, and party categories addressed practical requirements across different types of businesses.

### A foundation for product growth

Beyond the immediate UX improvements, the revamp addressed the product's longer-term foundations across mobile, web, and backend systems. The broader direction included capabilities such as multiple business profiles, multiple bank accounts, staff management, and enhanced reporting.

The goal was to make the product easier to use today while preparing it to support more complex business requirements in the future.

## Beta Testing: What We Learned About Real-World Usability

A product redesign doesn't end when the interface looks right. Beta testing surfaced issues that affected how users interacted with specific workflows, particularly transaction editing, quotations, payment reminders, and inventory selection.

Examples included:

- Payment status not updating correctly after editing a credit transaction.
- The need to create quotations without first adding a customer or supplier.
- Requests for shareable quotation links instead of PDF-only sharing.
- Inconsistent availability of quotation-to-sale conversion on web.
- Opportunities to improve transaction sharing and payment reminders.
- Requests for more predictable item selection and list sorting.

These findings highlighted an important distinction between feature availability and usability. A product may technically support a task, but if the next action is unclear, the status behaves unexpectedly, or sharing requires unnecessary steps, the experience still creates friction.

The feedback reinforced the need to validate complete user journeys rather than reviewing screens in isolation.

## Outcomes and Next Steps

The redesign established a more structured experience for business bookkeeping, financial monitoring, and everyday operations.

### What was delivered

- A simplified onboarding approach.
- A reorganized business dashboard.
- More accessible business reports.
- Quick transaction entry and shortcuts.
- Centralized notifications.
- Expanded transaction and quotation workflows.
- More flexible inventory and party management.
- A foundation for continued cross-platform product development.

### What still needs to be measured

The available project documentation does not establish quantified post-launch improvements in conversion, retention, transaction-entry speed, or business performance. Those outcomes need to be validated through product analytics and usability testing.

The next evaluation should focus on:

- **Onboarding conversion:** How many users complete registration and reach the homepage?
- **First transaction completion:** How many new users record their first business transaction?
- **Task completion time:** How long does it take users to record sales, purchases, and expenses?
- **Report engagement:** Are users finding and using financial reports more frequently?
- **Workflow reliability:** Do transaction editing, quotation sharing, and payment updates behave as expected?
- **Repeat usage:** Does the app help users maintain more consistent financial records?

These metrics would establish whether the redesigned experience delivers measurable improvements beyond visual and structural changes.

## Key Takeaway: Good Fintech UX Starts With Real Business Behavior

The most important lesson from the Karobar app redesign was that small business bookkeeping needs to fit into the way merchants actually work. A feature-rich accounting app can still feel difficult if onboarding creates unnecessary friction, financial information is hard to find, or routine transactions take too much effort.

Effective fintech UX design starts by understanding those barriers and building around real user needs. For Karobar, that meant simplifying onboarding, improving financial dashboard design, making reports easier to discover, and creating more flexible transaction and inventory workflows.

The goal wasn't to make every merchant use more features. It was to make the features they need easier to access and use consistently.

**The best bookkeeping app for small businesses isn't simply the one that tracks the most data. It's the one that makes maintaining accurate business records easier to sustain.**
